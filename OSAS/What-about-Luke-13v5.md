# What about Luke 13:5?

> "Unless you repent, you will likewise perish." - Luke 13:5 (Web)

Proponents of the Once Saved Always Striving (OSAS) gospel sometimes use this verse to say to the proponents of the Once Saved Always Saved (OSAS) gospel, "yes in John 3:16, the Lord said believe and you will have eternal life, but look, here it clearly indicates that repentance is necessary." But does it really indicate that?

## Three important questions

There are three questions we need to ask to properly understand what Jesus meant. First, who is this verse addressed to? Second, what does the word repent really mean in the context? And lastly, what does the word perish mean? Finally, as a bonus, is there a difference between Israel and the church in the plans of God? The answers to these questions will determine which side of the line this verse falls on when it comes to the question of OSAS.

## Scanning the audience for clues

First, the question of who Jesus was speaking to. It doesn't take any stretch of imagination from the context of the chapter and the context of that place in time, that Jesus whose earthly ministry was primarily to the lost sheep of Israel, was speaking to the people of his own nation, Israel. 

> Now there were some present at the same time... - Luke 13:1a

The above verse sets the context for the verse in the focus of this article. Jesus is speaking to these people who were present, and present at the same time. The question that naturally follows is what was that time and where is that there? These questions leads us to understand that the discourse is a continuation of what had already transpired in the previous chapter. So what really happened in the previous chapter and chapters?

In the context of Luke's narrative, in Luke 11, Jesus has openly and directly insulted the religious rulers of Israel and exposed their hypocrisy. They then became terribly angry and began looking for opportunities to catch him in his words (Luke 11:53-54). Then in the next verse, in the beginning of Luke 12 a multitude of many thousands gathered together, so many that they were trampling each other (Luke 12:1) and in that context Jesus begins comforting his own flock. "he began to tell his disciples first of all," warning them about the  hypocrisy of the Pharisees and teaching them many things. 

Then, one among the multitude tried to involve him in a family dispute, trying to make Him a judge, likely again trying to catch Him in His words. Jesus after asking him openly why he was trying to make him a judge, yet in graciousness gives a parable to the multitude warning against covetousness and selfishness, and then he again turns back to teaching the disciples ending His teaching with another parable about the coming of the Son of Man and ended with asking them to be ready. 

> Therefore be ready also, for the Son of Man is coming in an hour that you don’t expect him.” - Luke 12:40

Immediately then, in the next verse 41, Peter ever the inquisitive, questioning one asks Him:

> Peter said to him, “Lord, are you telling this parable to us, or to everybody?” - Luke 12:41

Why did Peter ask this question? What was in his mind? Clearly, Peter always the first to catch a glimpse of the spiritual realities being taught by the Lord had caught on to something. And the Lord while not directly answering his question, being the master teacher that he was, did give an answer in the rest of the discourse later on and in the overall thrust of the discourse. Notice:

> Do you think that I have come to give peace in the earth? I tell you, no, but rather division. For from now on, there will be five in one house divided, three against two, and two against three. They will be divided, father against son, and son against father; mother against daughter, and daughter against her mother; mother-in-law against her daughter-in-law, and daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law.”  - Luke 12: 51-53

It is after mentioning this division that He turns His attention from his disciples back to the multitudes:

> He said to the multitudes also, “When you see a cloud rising from the west, immediately you say, ‘A shower is coming,’ and so it happens. When a south wind blows, you say, ‘There will be a scorching heat,’ and it happens. You hypocrites! You know how to interpret the appearance of the earth and the sky, but how is it that you don’t interpret this time? “Why don’t you judge for yourselves what is right? - Luke 12: 54-57

So finally, after seeing the division between the disciples and the nation, we see that this is the context of the verse coming up, the verse which is the focus of this article. He is now going to answer these people whom He has understood to be, and labelled as hypocrites, in the next topic that they are baiting Him with. And that is the topic of tragedy they bring up in Luke 13. 
 
## What did he mean by repent? 

Repent (the verb metanoeō in the original) can be defined as changing one's frame of mind regarding something. Some define it as making a U-turn from a path one is pursuing. Now, in the context, we see whom Jesus is addressing, not His disciples but those in the multitude whom He has already labelled as hypocrites. In the larger context, they were a microcosm of the nation of Israel who would as a whole ultimately reject Him as their Messiah. On the surface, they were trying to make Him their judge, but they were only always trying to trap Him with their difficult questions.

So here is what He was saying, He was asking them to turn away from their hypocrisy. And this was their sin that led to their unbelief. It requires one to acknowledge sin before one understands one's need for a Saviour. The nation of Israel prided itself on their showy, outward, religious observances but had failed to understand that the very Son of God stood right in front of them. 

## What did He mean by perish?

> you will all perish in the same way. - Luke 13:5b

Finally, the third question. Jesus was warning the multitude of the nation of Israel that unless they repented of their ways of hypocrisy they would perish and in the same way. The part "in the same way" is important. In ancient times, towers were defensive structures. Archaeology has still not answered what the tower of Siloam was, but some scholars suggest that it might have been a Roman defensive structure. That said, and a little speculation here, but perhaps it could be that it was a foretaste of what would happen to Israel as a whole, when what they would trust in would come crashing down upon them. How so? 

The multitude had brought up the topic about Roman cruelty in Pilate's judgement. It is in this context that Jesus switches the incident they brought up, and rather brings up the tower of Siloam incident, which was not a sign of Roman judgement but of divine judgement. Later as we know, the Jews would eventually crucify Jesus by saying to Pilate "We have no king but Caesar," but then we also know the very king they hoped would defend them would fall on them and they would perish. Not only did the tower cause the pershing of those it fell on, it itself perished. We see that in the history of Israel and its dispersion, and in the consequent collapse and disintegration of the great Roman empire.

Thus, we know, sadly, the nation of Israel did not repent, and the accounts of the great Jewish historian Josephus, vividly describes the perishing of Jerusalem and the temple at the hands of the Roman empire just a few years  after they crucified Jesus. World history also widely details how the Roman empire eventually collapsed and was scattered like "iron mixed with clay". 

However, even that is only a minor foretaste to what is coming for the nation of Israel which still has not repented. And so, even more perishing is in store during the days of the tribulation, and the final manifestation of the "iron mixed with clay" empire, when the Jews will once again trust in the ruling Emperor, the Anti-Christ, until he betrays that trust.

## The difference between the church and Israel

Peter who had the "keys to the kingdom," clearly understood the difference between the call to repentance for the nation of Israel who had rejected Jesus as king and the call to believe for the church. His messages to the Jews, the Samaritans, and the gentiles after He was filled with the Holy Spirit on the day of Pentecost, was different. The call to the nation of Israel was to repent, but the call to the Samaritans and the gentiles was to believe. Do search for and read the article on this site "Peter's Keys and the Baptism of the Spirit." Paul too details the difference in the book of the Romans, while also saying that the nation of Israel will eventually, one day repent of their sin in crucifying their Messiah.  

# Conclusion

The gospel narrative is simple yet complex. So is the Bible. Understanding it rightly requires "properly handling the Word." 

> Give diligence to present yourself approved by God, a workman who doesn’t need to be ashamed, properly handling the Word of Truth. - 2 Tim 2:15. 

As I studied this, I for one am convinced which way this particular verse falls on the OSAS debate. Let us prayerfully seek the Lord's help and understanding so that we may be able to properly handle the Word of Truth. Amen!
