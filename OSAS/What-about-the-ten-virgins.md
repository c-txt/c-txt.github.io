# What about the ten virgins?

**Intro Note:** This writeup is part of a deep dive into the doctrines of Once Saved Always Saved (OSAS) and its counterpoint which could be called Once Saved Always Striving (OSAS). Which one is biblical is the question, and this series examines the relavant passages one-by-one in an attempt to figure out the answer. ## Will Jesus tell His bride, "I never knew you?"

The parable of the ten virgins in Mathew 25 has been interpreted in many ways, and is also often used to scare genuine believers into thinking they could lose their salvation. But is that really the case? Let's look at the passage in question:

> Then the kingdom of heaven will be like ten virgins who took their lamps and went to meet the bridegroom. Five of them were foolish, and five were wise. For when the foolish took their lamps, they took no oil with them, but the wise took flasks of oil with their lamps. As the bridegroom was delayed, they all became drowsy and slept. But at midnight there was a cry, ‘Here is the bridegroom! Come out to meet him.’ Then all those virgins rose and trimmed their lamps. And the foolish said to the wise, ‘Give us some of your oil, for our lamps are going out.’ But the wise answered, saying, ‘Since there will not be enough for us and for you, go rather to the dealers and buy for yourselves.’ And while they were going to buy, the bridegroom came, and those who were ready went in with him to the marriage feast, and the door was shut. Afterward the other virgins came also, saying, ‘Lord, lord, open to us.’ But he answered, ‘Truly, I say to you, I do not know you.’ Watch therefore, for you know neither the day nor the hour. - Matt 25: 1-13 (WEB)

Some say, that the five foolish virgins represent those in the church who are not ready spiritually to meet the Lord. But is this really the case?

## Who really are the virgins?

To rightly interpret this parable, one must look at its context, and for that we must turn back a chapter to the questions the disciples asked the Lord, which caused him to give them this parable:

> As he sat on the Mount of Olives, the disciples came to him privately, saying, “Tell us, when will these things be, and what will be the sign of your coming and of the end of the age?” - Matt 24:3	

It is in response to these questions that the Lord begins what is commonly known as his "Olivet discourse," because of where it was delivered, and He gives the parable of the ten virgins towards the end of this discourse. 

What you will notice is that the disciples were concerned about the destruction of the temple in Jerusalem that the Lord had just foretold. They were confused, because in their minds they were all about the Lord Jesus' kingdom program, and all they could visulaize was how and when He was going to set up His earthly rule. The mysteries of the intervening "church years" before that ever happened would only be understood slowly by them, through the heavenly vision given to Peter and then expanded upon by the revelation of the mystery of the church given to Paul.

And so the Olivet discourse focuses primarily upon what would happen to Israel, before the second coming of the Christ to set up His earthly rule. That this discourse refers to Israel is manifestly clear when you read many of the things He pointed to, such as the tribulation, the gospel of the kingdom, the abomination of desolation spoken of by the prophet Daniel, and the coming of the Son of Man. The parable of the ten virgins, part of the series of parables given in this discourse, therefore are referring to Israel as the ten virgins.

## Understanding the timeline through culture

It is not difficult to resolve some seeming dificulties about this passage when one considers a marriage in an Eastern context. Even today, in our own culture, a marriage feast often follows the actual wedding, and sometimes is arranged a short while after, and sometimes in a different location. The marriage may be conducted in a holy place such as a church, and the reception or marriage feast in a more public location, such as a hotel or convention hall to facilitate a large attendance. Even in ancient Jewsih culture it seems the marriage feast would follow only after the consummation of the marriage, with the actual wedding celebrations often lasting upto seven days.

Now look at Revelation 19 which talks about the mariage of the Lamb:

> Let us rejoice and exult
and give him the glory,
for the marriage of the Lamb has come,
and his Bride has made herself ready; - Rev 19:7

A few verses later, we see:

> Then I saw an angel standing in the sun, and with a loud voice he called to all the birds that fly directly overhead, “Come, gather for the great supper of God, to eat the flesh of kings, the flesh of captains, the flesh of mighty men, the flesh of horses and their riders, and the flesh of all men, both free and slave, both small and great. - Rev 19:17-18

The marriage of the Lamb to His bride the church will be in heaven, with the festivities lasting through the seven years of the tribulation, and the wedding supper will be on the earth likely referring to the millenial kingdom after armageddon where Israel too, as a nation, will become part of the kingdom. The Lord himself gives us a sense of this timeline when he says that He will return for judgement after the wedding:

> Stay dressed for action and keep your lamps burning, and be like men who are waiting for their master to come home from the wedding feast, so that they may open the door to him at once when he comes and knocks. - Luke 12:35-36

## A misapplication of scripture

As noted earlier, those who teach salvation can be lost often misapply the parable of the ten virgins. They fail firstly to note that the passage applies to Israel, secondly they fail to understand the timeline, and lastly fail also to note that the five virgins to whom the Lord said that He did not know them, never had any oil with them. They had lamps, but no oil. In other words they had the appearance of being ready, but were not ready in actuality. The oil respresents the Holy Spirit, which does the work of conviction and regeneation and the foolish have absolutely no knowledge of Him.    

There is much more to be said and examined when it comes to the prophecies of the Lord, as well as other prophecies concering future things that are yet to come. Understanding these requires careful study and meditation and the search of many scripture passages. This is why we would do well to be mindful of the apostle Peter's exhortation:

> We have the more sure word of prophecy; and you do well that you heed it as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star arises in your hearts, knowing this first, that no prophecy of Scripture is of private interpretation. - 2 Pet 1: 19,20

Prophecy that is of private interpretation relies on a single passage of text, and moulds it to fit preconceived notions. Rather, one who truly seeks to understand patiently compares scripture against scripture, relying on the Holy Spirit and right teachers of the Word of God, until the morning star arises. May that be true in our lives as well, and may we avoid the pitfalls of incorrect eschatological understandings that many fall into.
